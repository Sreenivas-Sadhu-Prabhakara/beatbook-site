Stop 01

The route is real. The record isn’t.

A distributor salesman or route-sales rep does the same honest thing every day: walk a fixed set of shops, take orders, and chase what’s owed. The problem was never the walking. It’s that the record of the day lives in three broken places.

  • The diary

    Yesterday’s dues are on a page he has to flip back to find. Ink smudges. A page tears. The book stays in the bag when the boss asks for numbers.

  • WhatsApp

    Orders and “paid 2000 sir” float in a chat that scrolls away. No shop-wise total, no closing balance — just messages nobody can add up at day end.

  • Memory

    “Balaji still owes something… I think.” The outstanding lives in one person’s head. He’s off sick, and the route goes blind.

So dues age quietly, a shop gets skipped, and at month-end nobody can say who owes what. The money isn’t lost to fraud — it’s lost to not being written down in one place.

Stop 02 · The wedge

A salesman’s day is two things at once: a route of shops, and a ledger of dues. Software keeps treating them separately. BeatBook is the one book that keeps both — the beat and the balance — on the same page.

Plan the beat by the day of the week. Capture the order at the visit. Let the visit move the balance. That’s the whole product, and it’s exactly how the job already works.

Stop 03

Four steps, and it’s the day he already has.

  1. 1

    Today’s beat builds itself

    Each retailer has a beat day. Open the app and today’s list is every shop whose beat day is today — already in route order. No planning meeting.

  2. 2

    Capture the order at the visit

    At each shop, punch the order. The amount is added to that retailer’s outstanding on the spot — the balance moves as the day moves.

  3. 3

    Log the collection, dues drop

    He collects Rs 2,000 from Balaji? Enter it and Balaji’s outstanding falls by exactly that. The running balance is always current, never end-of-day guesswork.

  4. 4

    Close the day with one glance

    Planned vs visited, coverage %, total outstanding, today’s collection — the coverage and collection reports write themselves from the visits.

Does he need to plan the route every morning?

No. Each shop is tagged with a beat day when you add it. Today’s beat is simply every shop whose beat day is today — it’s ready when he opens the app.

How does the outstanding stay correct?

It moves with the work. Booking an order adds to a retailer’s outstanding; logging a collection subtracts from it. There’s no separate ledger to reconcile at night.

What does the owner see at day end?

Coverage (planned vs visited), total outstanding across the route, and the day’s collection — shop-wise. The two reports the boss asks for, without anyone tallying a diary.
